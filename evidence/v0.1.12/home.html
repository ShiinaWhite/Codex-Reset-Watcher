LIMITS(5)

How Codex usage limits work

the 5-hour window

The short clock. Local messages (CLI, IDE extension, desktop app) and cloud chats draw from one shared 5-hour budget — they are not separate allowances. When your Codex CLI reports this window, /status shows its percentage left and reset time; use the rows your own client actually prints.

the weekly window

The ceiling behind it, running seven days. OpenAI has never published how large it is for any plan — the official pricing page says only that "additional weekly limits may apply". Clearing a 5-hour window does not restore weekly budget; the two refill independently.

what a reset actually is

The reset time is not computed on your machine. OpenAI's servers send an absolute resets_at timestamp with each usage report, and the CLI just renders it. A reset restores your percentage to 100% — it doesn't add credits or change your plan. Community reports suggest each window's clock starts on your first request after a reset, though OpenAI hasn't confirmed that.

goodwill & banked resets

Separately from scheduled windows, OpenAI may apply an automatic reset or save a banked reset for later. A banked grant does not refresh usage; redeeming a full banked reset resets both 5-hour and weekly windows. Referral resets normally use a 30-day window unless the offer says otherwise; every grant follows the expiry shown by its offer or account.

5-hour limit status: OpenAI lifted the 5-hour restriction for paid plans on Jul 12, 2026 and brought it back for Plus accounts on Aug 26, 2026 (announced by Tibo a day ahead to smooth compute load). Pro and Business have no matching announcement; the pricing page lists 5-hour estimates for every model, GPT-6 Astra included — treat your own /status output as the live truth.

FAQ

Codex reset questions, answered

When does my Codex 5-hour limit reset?

Your Codex client shows the windows currently returned for your account. Run /status in the Codex CLI and read the percentage left and reset time from the limit-window rows it actually prints. If a 5-hour limit (quota) row appears, enter that row's reset time in the tracker above; if it does not, do not infer one from this page.

When does the Codex weekly limit reset?

Seven days on its own clock, shown in the "Weekly limit" row of /status for your weekly quota. There is no universal weekly reset moment shared by everyone: community reports consistently indicate each account's window anchors to its own first request after the previous reset. That's why this tracker asks for your reset time instead of pretending one global countdown fits all.

How big is the weekly limit on my plan?

OpenAI does not publish it. The official pricing page says only "additional weekly limits may apply" and gives no weekly number for any plan — anyone quoting an exact weekly cap is guessing. What is documented: the weekly window runs seven days and refills independently of the 5-hour window.

Did Codex reset today?

Check the radar above — it watches @thsottiaux (the Codex lead, who announces virtually every goodwill reset), flags rate limit reset announcements automatically, and shows how long ago the last one landed. OpenAI has granted 38 recorded global resets since September 2025, including 10 in the twelve days after GPT-5.6 launched, and the GPT-6 Astra rollout (Sep 3–5, 2026) added a banked reset for every day a paid account lacked Astra plus a full banked reset for all Plus, Pro and Business users once it reached everyone.

Does clearing my 5-hour window restore my weekly limit?

No. They are independent budgets refilling on separate schedules. If your weekly window is exhausted, a fresh 5-hour window won't unblock you — you need the weekly rollover, a goodwill reset, a banked reset, or credits.

Are Codex limits separate from my ChatGPT chat limits?

Yes, but Codex isn't alone in its pool: Codex, ChatGPT Work, ChatGPT for Excel and Workspace Agents all share one agentic usage pool. Ordinary ChatGPT chat has separate caps and never drains Codex budget — a heavy ChatGPT Work session will, though.

What is a banked reset, and when does mine expire?

A reset saved to your account until you choose to redeem it. The grant itself does not refresh usage; a full redemption resets both the 5-hour and weekly windows and moves the weekly date to roughly seven days later. Referral resets normally must be used within 30 days unless the offer says otherwise, while milestone, compensation, and one-time grants follow the expiry shown by the account or offer. Read the banked reset guide

What time of day do Codex resets usually happen?

From the 50 recorded announcements: they cluster between 17:00 and 23:59 UTC — US afternoon — with a secondary bump just after midnight UTC. The histogram above localizes every announcement to your timezone. Day of week is nearly uniform, so don't bother planning around one.

What happens when I hit a limit mid-task?

OpenAI lets the agent finish the turn in progress (subject to fair use), then blocks new turns until a window clears. Your session and history stay put. Options: wait it out, spend a banked reset, buy credits (Plus/Pro: Settings → Usage → Credits; valid for 12 months), switch to a lighter model like GPT-5.6 Luna (its 5-hour estimate is roughly 50× Astra's), or move the task to an API key.

Can I dodge subscription limits with an API key?

Yes, with a trade-off: signing Codex in with an API key bills per token at standard API rates and removes the 5-hour and weekly windows entirely — but you lose the cloud features (GitHub code review, Slack, Codex cloud), and your only ceiling is the spend limit you configure.

Is the 5-hour limit still lifted?

Not for Plus. OpenAI removed it for paid plans on July 12, 2026 during the GPT-5.6 demand surge, then Tibo announced on August 25 that the 5-hour limit would return for Plus accounts across ChatGPT Work and Codex the next day, to smooth compute load. No matching return has been announced for Pro or Business. The pricing page publishes 5-hour estimates for every model, GPT-6 Astra included — check your own /status for the live answer, and watch the radar either way.

Does GPT-6 Astra have its own usage limit?

No separate meter. Astra is included in the normal allowance on every paid plan and you can spend 100% of it on Astra, but it is the most expensive model in the pool: OpenAI estimates 5–45 local messages per 5-hour window on Plus, against 10–100 for Sol and 250–2,000 for Luna, and Fast mode bills Astra at 2.5× its standard rate. During the Sep 3–5, 2026 rollout OpenAI banked one reset per day for paid accounts without Astra, then a full banked reset for all Plus, Pro and Business users.

Can this site see my real Codex usage?

No — this site never touches your OpenAI account. Your real numbers live in OpenAI's Codex and Work Analytics dashboard at https://chatgpt.com/codex/cloud/settings/analytics#usage or run `/status` in the CLI. OpenAI provides no public personal-quota API for this site, so your countdown, notifications, and calendar export still run only in your browser. The public announcement feed remains available at `/api/feed`.

Privacy

Effective 2026-08-30. Short version: your timer stays on your device. If you actively mark that your personal weekly window just reset, the site accepts a minimal anonymous observation to calibrate reset detection; it cannot confirm resets during this calibration period.

What stays local. Reset times and notification preferences are saved under the codexreset.v1 localStorage key. Your vigil membership — the cycle you prayed in, your sequence number, and your generated pilgrim name — is saved under codexreset.v1:pray. Those timer and vigil records are not uploaded as records; clearing browser data removes them from that browser, but it does not remove an already accepted prayer from the public cycle records.

Analytics. The site has no sign-up. It loads Cloudflare Web Analytics and Google Analytics 4 to understand aggregate site use. Google Analytics may set first-party analytics cookies and send page, browser, device, and network-derived information to Google; the tag is not used to sign visitors in. You can block or clear analytics cookies through your browser. The credited community video on /tibo is served as a same-origin static file, so playing it does not contact X; only following the original-post credit link leaves this site.

Server processing. The site runs on Cloudflare Workers; Cloudflare processes standard request metadata (IP, user agent) to serve and protect the site. The /api/pray endpoint accepts one prayer per person per reset cycle. To enforce that without keeping addresses, the Worker stores a salted one-way hash of the request IP for up to 45 days; the raw IP is never written into application records, responses, or the public page. The request IP itself is additionally used, transiently, by Cloudflare's rate-limit binding.

Reset calibration. The anonymous observation is sent only when you actively choose “My weekly window reset” and the tracker already has a personal reset time. The browser sends the action time, the remaining milliseconds on that previous personal window, and a random pseudonym that rotates each UTC day; it sends no account identifier or credentials. At the edge, the action time is rounded to a 15-minute bucket, the remaining value is reduced to a coarse range, and a one-way hash of the daily pseudonym is used in a deterministic key so at most one sample is retained for that pseudonym on that UTC day. The stored sample contains only that 15-minute bucket and coarse range and expires after 14 days. The exact action time, raw remaining value, raw pseudonym, and request IP are not written to application storage; Cloudflare's rate limiter uses the IP transiently. These observations are shadow data only and do not confirm or change a reset, forecast, alert, or public counter.

Notifications. In-page notifications are optional, fire only while the page is open, and can be revoked any time in your browser's site settings.

Push notifications. If you explicitly turn on push notifications, your browser creates a push subscription and this site stores the subscription endpoint URL it issues, the time you subscribed, your page language, and whether the active Service Worker can safely render the full alert scope. That endpoint is a per-device identifier; no name, email, account, IP address, or user agent is stored with it. Alert content does not travel through the push service: the push message carries no payload, and your browser fetches the latest selected details from this site when it wakes, so the wake is not a promise that every Event will render separately. Alert v3 owns post-fence regular Reset and strong Watch delivery; historical events at or below the cutover fence do not replay. Compatible subscriptions wake for a regular global Reset, an 83%/93% strong Watch, or a qualifying global banked lifecycle update on its separate banked route. Model-only forecasts and silent context do not notify. Turning notifications off in the control, or revoking the permission in your browser, deletes the stored subscription and compatibility marker; endpoints that the push service reports as expired are deleted automatically.

Email alerts. Email subscriptions are handled by the separate noindex companion at notify.codex-reset.com. It uses Cloudflare Turnstile and double opt-in. An address is HMAC-hashed for private lookup and encrypted with AES-GCM only while the request is pending or active; unsubscribe, hard bounce, complaint, or provider suppression clears the ciphertext immediately. Delivery receipts and provider event IDs exclude the address, and messages use no open or click tracking. The companion publishes its own Privacy and Terms pages.

Public data. The announcement radar republishes short excerpts of public posts for commentary and news reporting. An accepted prayer publishes a generated pseudonym, a sequence number, and a coarse location (country and, when available, city from Cloudflare's edge metadata) on the public vigil wall, alongside aggregate cycle totals. No surface publishes your IP address or any account identity.
